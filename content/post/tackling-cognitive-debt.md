Suppose your product suffered a product incident. The kind that is caused due to a programming bug or a flawed release. 
Your team went through the stages of mitigation, investigation, root cause analysis,
incident reports and follow-up actions etc. Once the dust is settled, how sure are you that everyone in your team familiarized themselves with
the root cause of this incident, such that they will not recreate the circumstances that can cause a repeat incident?

As much as agentic coding is analogous to human coding, what we have learnt is it is important to know what information is present in the agent's context. 
Same applies to people. The probability of a team causing a repeat incident depends on how familiar all members of the team are with the previous incident(s). 
So it important that past incidents and their root causes are firmly embedded in the team's communal memory.

Margaret-Anne Storey [popularized the term 'cognitive debt'](https://margaretstorey.com/blog/2026/02/09/cognitive-debt/) 
which means a loss of understanding or knowledge gaps that can emerge among the developers working on a software project.
This gap can emerge in spite of the usual collaborative processes like standups, design discussions, backlog refinement, pull request reviews,
sprint demo etc.

In a fast-paced environment, this is a common problem that affects all aspects of work.
